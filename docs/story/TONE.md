# OVERDUE tone guide

For everyone who writes or builds for OVERDUE. The owner's brief (2026-11-10):

> "This is a tale about mega corporations capitalism the after affects of bad and corrupt leadership that even after
> the world practically ended there will still be those who want to be on top at the expense of those beneath them ...
> I want players to be like solo is haunting and eerie. Body modifications have become the staple, if you aren't
> willing to kill and commit atrocities then you won't ever be able to make it to the top."

[CANON.md](script/CANON.md) and its reveal order come first, then this page. When a scene gets serious, copy mission 6
([TRAIN.md](TRAIN.md)): plain, specific, physical, no speeches. The jokes go quiet and nothing louder replaces them.
The first pass in this register: [TONE_PASS_ACT1.md](TONE_PASS_ACT1.md).

## 1. The four pillars

**1. The companies outlived the world.** Six boards signed Option C and launched at close of business so every wage,
pension and debt burned with it. All six are still open. The debt outlived the debtors: 244 quarters of back pay for a
job nobody had, collected in people, by Mr. Overtime and then a truck. Up the hill the Schedule rations air and light
to the minute and puts the late outside. What they left is still in the ground: the Gut is the war's dead, fused by
Regrowth (a product line, fired as a weapon); it is in the rain; the Off-Spec are born with it.
*On the page:* the horror talks like an office (policy, slot, balance carried forward). Nobody at the top is angry.
The cost is a number and the number is a person. Show it in objects (a slip on a door, a lanyard, a weight in a
Filofax), never in a speech.

**2. Alone, the city is haunted.** Section 3.

**3. Everybody is partly somebody else.** A new arm is a haircut here: Bev's waiting room is a barber's. Ten slots, a
Body Budget (3, +1 an act, +1 per three cats: a promotion is permission to hold more of other people), then Drift:
"you won't notice. the people who love you notice." The parts come off the trucks. Marion Hask is the far end of the
same road: 101, alive on donors' organs, reading their names from a ledger every night.
*On the page:* say who has what installed in passing; nobody stares. An install is never only a power-up.

**4. Nobody reaches the top clean.** Section 2.

## 2. The ladder (shown through play, never a lecture)

The game never says power corrupts. It hands over the next rung and shows who it is made of.

**What climbing asks (all existing)**
- *Parts from people.* The Heart Rip (m04): your first part comes out of a man who is a recording like you. Every boss
  core comes out the same way. The shop shelf comes off the same trucks as the Bottom's missing. Jack's marrow (m06):
  the fight cannot be won without it and the player presses the button.
- *Deals.* DEAL or TAKEOVER in each company district; the Bishop keeps Dot "as collateral"; Evergreen left waving buys
  a militia; Hask's offer; Lumps' letters to Pritchard.
- *Who is collected instead of you.* Dot stood in front of the truck that came for Lumps. Nine go while you fight
  Overtime, then you pick the truck or your friend. Sister Agenda takes the blame in QUARTERLY GROWTH unless you go
  back. Taking the Gut frees the Bottom's people and kills the old digesters.

**Where the player may refuse, and the cost**
- Any part after the first: staying in budget keeps Drift 0-1 (Cale's memories, the warm lines) and costs perks.
  Lumps is the model: he said no to Bev and built his rig on the outside.
- m19's top grade is "you declined every upgrade"; SPARE PARTS and BUY BACK each have a "leave it" that pays nothing.
- Any deal: refusing one loses the discount and the shaky ally, and makes that company your enemy in its district.
- The one rung with no refusal is Jack. Keep it the only one.

**How the meters carry it.** `bottom` standing moves when you pay yourself (the honest answer in m01, staying with
Lumps in m04); `corp` moves with deals. Drift: cold lines from 2, no memory flashes at 4+, and Hask's "You would make
a perfect employee." ON TIME is the top of the ladder: Overtime's job, your name on a lanyard, ten thousand copies of
you, the Bottom sold.

**PROPOSED (small, not built)**
1. **PROPOSED: tape on the jar.** Once the player has `learned_feedstock`, shop implant cards show a strip of tape: a
   date and a truck number. Hask's tagged organs in m32 use the same tape.
2. **PROPOSED: somebody pays the fee.** Hide from ★3+ Late Fees inside the Bottom and the collectors take the nearest
   door instead. It stands open with a slip on it until m20.
3. **PROPOSED: the crayon line.** Review cards are the company grading you. Where a person was taken or killed, one
   line of Auntie's crayon under the grade says who paid ("nine.").

## 3. Solo is haunting

When the player has no co-op partner and no companion. Company or a fight brings the score and the banter back.
1. **Sound.** No music in the open: room tone, and you. Your body is the loudest thing in the street, and the tick
   comes back off the walls a little late.
2. **Emptiness.** More places than people: bowls, chairs, doors. Count them. A number is worse than an adjective.
3. **Unwatched.** People who think nobody is looking drop the bit: Auntie says something twice to the cold soup; Dot
   tells a face in the wall what you were wearing. One per hub scene. Nobody mentions it.
4. **What is left.** Where someone was collected: the door open, a ☺ CORP slip stapled at head height (COLLECTED, a
   name, a time to the second, BALANCE CARRIED FORWARD), dinner on the table. No blood. Collection is tidy, and that
   is what is wrong with it.
5. **Nothing jumps.** Things are wrong and hold still. The player finds them; the camera does not point.

## 4. Comedy and horror in one scene

The deadpan stays. It stops being safe.
- Keep the joke's shape. Make its last clause a fact.
- Nobody laughs and nobody explains. The worst thing gets the flattest sentence.
- Jokes aim up: the companies, the system, the speaker. Never the dead, the collected or the Off-Spec.
- Plant a habit as a joke and let it hurt later (Jack's weights).
- At a death or a choice the jokes stop (m06's card: NO REVIEW TODAY).

## 5. Hard limits

- **Canon first.** Nothing contradicts CANON.md or its reveal order. In Act I the player learns *what* ☺ CORP does to
  people, not *why*, and not who the parts go to.
- **Everything is original.** No real companies, people or events.
- **Cruelty has weight and is never a reward gag.** No loot shower, HUD callout, kill-feed joke or review-card
  punchline for hurting someone who cannot fight back. If the player profits, the scene shows who paid.
- **Children are not harmed on screen.** They are put back (the baby in C4). They lose people; that is enough.
- **The war is never the punchline**, and the Off-Spec are people first ([DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) §7.1).
- **It must work with gore off.** The Squeamish toggle swaps flesh for foam. The horror lives in the slip on the door,
  not in the blood.
