## 1. Who she is

The sign says GRISTLE & SONS. Augustus had one son and one daughter, and the sign was painted before he counted.
Cordelia was Company Secretary, then Head of Trials, then Chair, because somebody had to hold the meeting while her
brother Cornelius was learning to sing.

On the Final Quarter the board was in the campus boardroom for the 16:59 call. Regrowth came up through the floor that
week. The five non-executive directors fused where they sat, round the table, into one animal with a brim. She was
chairing. It would not be chaired by anyone it could not watch, so it took her eyes and kept them. She let it, on one
condition: it sits where she can carry it.

For the first years the Gut digested everything alive that stood on it. She found the rule, in Trials, the way she
found everything: on other people. **The Gut does not digest its own heart.** A heart cut out and put into the ground
alive is taken up, and the ground keeps that body running as part of itself. The first forty did not take. The
forty-first was her own, and she did it awake. The bone tower came up under it in a season. Nobody built it.

Then she sold the only thing worth having after the end of the world. *Live here and you will not die.* The price is
one heart. Sixty-one years; the Big Heart is about one thousand seven hundred of them, and hers is the one in the
middle that the rest grew round.

## 2. The heart arrangement (the spine of her power)

- **The buy-in.** To live in the Gut you leave your heart with her. Literally: it is cut out and goes into the Big
  Heart. Figuratively: you are hers, and she says so kindly. The feedstock off the trucks does not get asked.
- **The cap.** Every resident has a brass cap the size of a coin left of the breastbone, stamped with the house mark
  and a number. Residents show it like a pass. Hers is number 1 and has no cap on it: a brass-ringed socket in the V of
  her lapels, dark, empty, on show.
- **Nobody dies.** A killed body is regrown where it fell, or walks home by morning. The Choir singers who "get up
  again, unhurt" in the Bishop's fight, and the planned respawning civilians, are this. It is also the business: a body
  that regrows can be harvested for ever. The good organs go up the hill. The hides go into her collection.
- **The only real death** is your own heart destroyed in the Big Heart.
- **Why nobody shoots it.** Every heart lost from it ends one person, and from the ground you cannot tell whose. It
  might be a Choir bass. It might be Bev. It might be Dot. Some of the hearts are the war's dead, and when one of those
  goes a street turns grey and stops breathing.
- **Hers is the centre.** Destroy it and the whole heart fails: every resident, every digester, the ground. That is
  why nobody in sixty-one years has moved against her, and why she can afford to be polite.
- **Visitors.** A day in the Gut costs nothing. The Gut starts on you in weeks. She offers every visitor the same
  thing, once, and means it.
- **You.** Bev opened your chest in m05: a bracket and a stock number. Then she put a piece of Mr. Overtime's
  punch-clock in it. You are the one person in her district she has nothing of. She finds this interesting.
- **Who is in charge.** Neither. Oversight holds her eyes: it can blind her by shutting two lids. She holds
  Oversight's five hearts, round hers, at the centre. It is a board that cannot sack its chair and a chair that cannot
  dissolve her board. It is hungry all the time. She has the casting vote.

## 3. Lord of life and death, and of fashion

One business. **A body is the first thing you wear.** She holds the pattern (your heart), so she can cut you another,
and the house decides the cut. People who die in the Gut come back *altered*: this season's shoulders, the house's
idea of a waist. Nobody asked for the alterations. Nobody is charged.

- Her suit is grown, not woven: patent hide, raised on the back of a named resident, who gets his back in the autumn.
- The district dresses like her a season late. Wide shoulder bars on the Choir, on stallholders, on a child.
- An arm is a haircut here ([TONE.md](../TONE.md) pillar 3). She is why. Bev does repairs; the house does couture.
- She calls the cap "the label".
- You are a mannequin: a thing clothes are shown on. She has wanted one for years.

**The two lords at her gate.** Mr. Overtime oversees work and time ([CANON.md](../script/CANON.md), The Lords). By
contract his collectors have right of way from the Gut Border Gate to the Feed Dock at four, and for that hour one
road in her district keeps his time. She tolerates the trucks: they are her supply. She does not tolerate the clock.
He *terminates* people on that road, and a termination is a death she did not give. She cannot hold him (no heart: a
clock), cannot kill him (he is reloaded from file), and cannot refuse the trucks. In sixty-one years nobody has taken
anything out of him that stayed out. Then you did, and Bev put it in your chest on her side of the gate.

## 4. How they move and speak

**She** barely moves. Hands a little out from her sides, fingers down, too long by a joint. She ducks nothing: the
hat tilts through the doorway and she follows it. She walks like a wading bird, knee first, no hurry. Her face stays
level and polite, aimed slightly over your head, because she is not looking at you with it. She offers you things:
a seat, a sweet, a place. Lowercase, quiet, board procedure and hospitality. Never angry.

**Oversight** breathes. The brim rises and settles, the growths pulse out of time, five odd eyes blink when they
like. The brown pair blinks together and looks at whoever she is talking to: the hat tips to aim them while her chin
stays where it is. It has one word, said by several voices at once: **"aye."**

> do sit. no? then i shall stand, and you may look up.
> nobody dies in my district, dear. i don't allow it.
> you're thinner than the minutes said.
> i have your friend's mother. i have everybody's mother. it's a kind of family.
> oversight would like to eat you. that's noted. it isn't carried.
> i don't see you at all. oversight sees you. i'm told you're plastic.
> shoulders are wide this year. you'll notice it on the people who've died.
> you may shoot it, if you like. choose who.

## 6. Act II: a larger role

1. **A standing invitation.** The train is yours now, and it stops under the Gut whether you ask or not. A stair of
   ribs, tea at the foot of the tower, the hat held level. She tells you what the Big Heart is, plainly, and hands you
   the choice in the last sample line. *Reveal: the arrangement. Hers is not named yet.*
2. **She uses you.** She knows where everyone is; that is all a register is. She points you at pages 1 to 3 because
   each one ruins a competitor, and at Overtime's second fight because it suits her. She keeps quiet about page 4. It
   was stolen from her.
3. **She protects you.** Late Fees ★4, collectors on your heels to the Gut Border Gate. She is standing on the
   landing. The brim opens to its full three metres. "this one is a guest." One collector steps over the line. He is
   back on his feet by morning, on her side, with a cap, working the Feed Dock.
4. **The withdrawal.** Wristband names Mina; you ask for her. "she's on the register." A heart can be released. She
   has never done it. The price is a heart she does not hold. Lumps offers his for Dot before you can speak.
5. **The turn (default: she is betrayed).** Gordo fires one shot into the Big Heart to prove it can be hurt. A named
   person stops in the street. Her voice does not change. "that was one of mine." The Gut closes to you until m20.
   *Alternative: she sells you. Hask is her best customer, and you were never on her books.*
6. **The dilemma, said once.** The only way to stop her is the one in the middle. Sister Agenda has the count in the
   minutes: one thousand seven hundred and twelve, and the ground.

## 8. Inspirations, and how she differs

The owner named Lady Dimitrescu and the Other Mother. Taken, for feel: a hostess who towers over a small guest and
is in no hurry; courtesy that could end in a second; warmth that pays slightly too much attention; a thin body that
is less human the closer you stand; "mine". Not taken: no white dress, no rose, no claws from the fingers, no
daughters, no buttons, no needles, thread or webs, neither face, neither outfit. What is hers alone: she is a
chairwoman in a grown suit; the monster is her board and it is on her head; the eyes she looks at you with are not in
her face; and the threat is not what she will do to you. It is the arithmetic of what happens if you win.

## 9. Gameplay hooks (not built)

  callout (`KEN ABARA. LIVER. STOPPED.` No brag styling, no loot).

## 10. Open questions (defaults in bold)

1. **What is "in her way"?** **Mr. Overtime** (the rival lord at her gate; you are after him all game, and you carry
   the one piece of him that stayed out). Alternative: Revenue Protection, the thing in the Loop under her house that
   wears the company's face and has four hundred hearts she does not hold.
2. **Can she be killed without the massacre?** **Not in Act II: that is the point.** Later, one way: something that
   never stops has to go in where hers was. The Cracked Punch-Clock would do it, and the Gut would start keeping
   time. Sister Agenda would offer hers.
3. **Who is on the register?** **Everyone who sleeps on the Gut side: the Choir, the traders, Bev, the feedstock.**
   This makes m20's rescue a withdrawal, not a rope.
4. **Is she a Gristle, and above the Bishop?** **Yes to both** (elder sister, Chair over CEO). Alternative: no
   relation, and the Bishop tolerates her because his heart is in the tower too.
