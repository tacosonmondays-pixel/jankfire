# Mechs: research (the feel we're after, and how we keep it ours)

The owner asked us to look at Super Mecha Champions (the mechs Skylark and Arthur) and Gundam for the feel. This is a
summary of that research and what the two JANKFIRE frames take from it. The fandom wiki is blocked on our network, so
it comes from search summaries; nothing below is copied from any game's art, and no asset, name, logo or shape was
taken. Rule from CLAUDE.md: inspired by, never copied.

## Super Mecha Champions (NetEase, 2019, now delisted)
- An anime battle royale in a manga-styled city. You play a pilot; after a cooldown you **summon your mech**, it
  **drops in** from the sky and you **hop in**. About ten mechs, each with its own kit, skins and graffiti. Mechs had
  durability, boosts, shields and transforms. One later mech was designed by Shoji Kawamori.
- **Skylark**: a mobility / poke mech. An energy rifle; an "energy catapult" that launches it at an enemy and heals
  it from the damage dealt; a wing that detaches as an attacking bird; an air skill for speed. Its design mixed a
  traditional Japanese family-crest motif with modern tech.
- **Arthur**: the ace knight. Boosters on the back and legs, a giant sword that controls the fight; the blade stacks
  marks and paralyses on the 4th stack; a three-hit charging slash.

## Mobile-suit design language (general mecha, Kunio Okawara's school)
- Functional, military machines with visible mechanical detail; humanoid proportions; a heroic, face-like head;
  every joint placed with intent; bulk and a low centre of gravity for heavies.
- A white base with bold colour blocking; chest intakes and vents, skirt armour, backpack thrusters and leg
  verniers, a shield, sword hilts stowed on the back.

## The anime feel (animation, phase 2)
- Missile salvos that spiral out on twisting smoke trails, swarm, then track fast (the "Itano circus").
- Snappy key poses held for a beat, overshoot, smears, low heroic camera angles, glowing blade arcs, dust rings on
  landing.

## What we avoid (signatures of other works)
- No V-fin antenna and no pair of "Gundam eyes" (Gundam's signature); no mono-eye (Zaku's).
- No Super Mecha Champions names, crests, colours or silhouettes. Our frames share roles (a flyer, a knight) and
  general mecha language, not designs.

## How each frame uses it
**PAPER PLANE** (the flyer, our answer to "a mobility mech with a wing bird")
- Origami motif: crisp folded-panel armour with crease lines, a paper plane's nose for a chest, a swept folded-paper
  crest on the helmet, and a **split shutter visor** (three glowing slats either side of a white keel; no eyes).
- A **folded-crane emblem** on the chest (our own mark, not a family crest).
- **Wing drone**: a little **origami crane** docked on the left wing (its own joint, `crane`). It peels off, pecks
  the target and comes back.
- **Lunge-and-heal boost** (gameplay: a dash at an enemy that heals from the damage).
- **Big move**: an Itano-style missile salvo of homing pencils on spiralling trails.
- **Jet mode** folds it like a paper plane (wings swing down flat into a dart).

**NIGHT SHIFT** (the knight, our answer to "an ace with a giant sword")
- A knight crossed with a night-shift worker: a great helm with a **hard-hat ridge and peak**, one glowing **visor
  bar**, a spinning security beacon, hi-vis bands, a badge, a **timecard** stuck in a slot on the chest.
- **Back and leg boosters** (two big backpack thrusters, verniers low on each calf).
- The energy-edged snap-off sword: it stamps **OVERTIME** marks; the 4th one **clocks them out** (a stun).
  **TRIPLE SHIFT** is three dashing slashes, plus the big overhead ground slam.
- The **PLEASE HOLD** tower shield.

**Both**: a visible inner frame at the joints, skirt armour, backpack thrusters, verniers, panel lines and caution
decals (general mecha language), the drop-in from the sky and the pilot climbing in.
