## What to take from it (the art session's read)
1. **Clean.** This is the owner's point. The surfaces are big and smooth, with glossy car-paint highlights and almost no grime or scratches. Panel lines are few, purposeful and crisp, and rivets are rare: one hex-bolt cluster as a feature, not rivets everywhere. Detail comes from shape breaks, white trim stripes and a few small decals, not surface noise.
2. **Two or three colours per mech, high contrast.** One main paint, white, dark joints, and one glow colour.
3. **Crisp graphic accents.** Thin white or coloured trim stripes that follow the armour edges, a unit number, a small logo or triangle mark, chevrons. Our hero-kit stickers fit this, kept small and neat.
4. **Glows are few and strong.** Eyes or visor slits, one status light, energy edges on wings and blades.
5. **Character.** Each mech reads as a personality: the mascot bear, the dragon knight, the canopy pilot. That fits our VENDING MACHINE / TROLLEY / WRECKING BALL.
6. **Pilots are visible:** a canopy you can see into, and the pilot standing next to the mech.

## What changes on our side (mech art)
- The painted-metal look gets a **clean** setting, the default for mechs: the tiling grime, scratches and chips go nearly to zero and the panel lines are kept light; worn edges are only a soft highlight. The tiling set stays for props.
- Fewer rivets: hex-bolt clusters as features instead.
- Thin white trim stripes on the armour edges.
- WRECKING BALL / VENDING MACHINE / TROLLEY are built clean from the start. PAPER PLANE / NIGHT SHIFT get rebuilt clean in a small follow-up PR.
